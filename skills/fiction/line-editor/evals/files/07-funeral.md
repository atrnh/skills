The church was too warm and smelled of lilies and somebody's hairspray. Mara sat at the end of the pew with her hands folded the way Mama had folded them for her in the car.

Uncle Ray had flown in from Tulsa that morning and still had the airport on him and kept checking his watch as if Daddy might run long.

The pastor talked about fishing. Daddy had not fished once in Mara's whole life but the pastor had a story about a lake and a boy and a borrowed rod. Mara stopped listening somewhere around the rod and counted the pipes on the organ instead and got to thirty-one and lost her place and started over.

Then the hymn. Then Uncle Ray and five men from the plant came forward in their church shoes.

When they lifted the casket tipped toward Uncle Ray's end and for one long second Mara thought it would go over and Daddy would roll out onto the carpet in his good suit and everyone would have to look at him.

It didn't. Uncle Ray got his shoulder under it. The men found their step and carried Daddy down the aisle slow and level. Nobody else in the pews seemed to have seen.

Mama touched her elbow. "Breathe, baby. Just breathe."

Mara breathed and followed the casket out into light so flat and white it hurt. The hearse was already running.
