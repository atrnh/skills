The house had stood empty since the funeral in '31, and Ruth could smell it before she reached the porch: damp, and something sweeter underneath, like fruit left in a drawer.

"You know Daddy's been dead three years this March," June said behind her. "Nobody's going to have kept it up."

Ruth didn't answer. She climbed the steps with the kerosene lamp. With the lamp held in both hands, she turned the key and pushed the door open.

Inside, the wallpaper had begun to blister along the seams. June came in after her, stamping snow off off her boots, and Ruth told her sister she had left the back gate open. It was not the kind of thing she forgot.

They stood in the front room while the flame steadied. A blister of damp had spread across the ceiling above the stove, brown at the edges. The sweet smell came form upstairs.

Climbing the stairs, the smell grew stronger. At the top, Ruth stopped. Fear was a cold hand at her throat, and she swallowed it down.

Their father's door stood open the width of a finger. She pushed it with her boot. The bed was made. Someone had turned down the quilt and set a glass of water on the night table, and the water was fresh.

She needed a minute to process it. June said her name twice before she heard.

"The gate," June said. "Ruth. Who opened the gate?"
