The tide is out, and the flats run grey out to the channel markers. Tessa walks them barefoot because Aunt Del told her to, because shoes are how the drownfolk know you're a stranger.

"Don't go past the posts," Del calls from the seawall. "And don't go pickin' anything up. Nothin'. Not even if it's pretty."

The posts are old pilings, black and soft with rot. Past them the mud shines like a mirror, like a knife blade, like the skin on warm milk.

The tidewife's house stands at the end of the causeway, shuttered, its grey paint peeling in long curls. Tessa has been told three times not to look at it. She looks at it.

Something moves behind the shutters.

"I'm comin' back," she calls, but the wind takes it, and when she turns, the seawall is empty.
