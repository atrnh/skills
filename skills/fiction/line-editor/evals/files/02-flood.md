The water was coming up. Mama said it wouldn't, not past the second step, not in April, and Mama was wrong about the river maybe once in her whole life and this was the once.

The water was coming up.

I stood on the porch in my sock feet and watched it take the garden. Beans first. Then the tomato cages, leaning like drunks. Then the fence Pa built the year I was born, which was a good fence, which was the best fence on Cold Creek Road, and which went under like it never was.

Pa was down in the barn with the mare. He was thinking he ought to have sold her in March, when Dell Tuttle offered.

"Get them shoes on," Mama said. "We ain't got all night."

I thought about my shoes. I thought about the mare. I thought about the jar of buttons under my bed that was Grandma's and that I was not supposed to have, and how buttons would sink, and how Grandma would of known what to do, and how she was dead, and how the dead don't come when you call them no matter how loud, and how the water was coming up the steps now, one, two, the third one gone while I stood there thinking about buttons.

Brown water. Cold. A whole chicken coop riding it like it was nothing.

"Shoes," Mama said.

I got my shoes. I got the buttons too. When I came back out, Pa is leading the mare up the hill with the rope wrapped twice around his fist, and the water was coming up.
