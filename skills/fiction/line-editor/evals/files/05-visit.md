Marisol parked behind her brother's truck and sat for a moment while the engine ticked. She had practiced her opening the whole drive up from Fresno, out loud, until it stopped sounding like their mother: *I'm not asking you to like it. I'm asking you to sign.*

The screen door had a new latch. She knocked.

Danny came to the door in his work socks, holding a dish towel. He looked at her for a moment, then past her at the car, as if checking whether she'd brought anyone.

"You should've called," he said.

"I did. Four times."

He stepped back to let her in.

The kitchen smelled of onions and bleach. The table where they had done their homework as kids was covered in feed-store receipts, and the calendar by the phone still showed their mother's handwriting in the squares for June: *Dr. Patel, 2:30. Church potluck. Call Mari.* Marisol stood in front of the calendar for a moment, then turned her back on it.

"Coffee?" Danny asked.

"Sure."

He let the tap run cold before he filled the kettle, the way their father used to, and she almost said so. Instead she sat at the table and moved a stack of receipts to make room for her folder. She had bought the folder at a gas station in Merced because the papers had seemed too thin on their own, and now she looked like she'd come to sell him insurance.

Danny set a mug in front of her and stayed standing, the dish towel over his shoulder. "If this is about the house, the answer's still no."

"I'm not asking you to like it," she said.

They argued for most of an hour, and he signed only after she told him about the second mortgage.

On the drive back she kept the radio off. Somewhere past Merced she realized she was still holding his pen, its cap chewed flat. For a moment she thought about throwing it out the window. She drove the rest of the way with the pen in her fist.
