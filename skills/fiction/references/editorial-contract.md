# Editorial contract

Use this contract whenever a skill reads, records, or reviews fiction. Skills that review a manuscript also follow [review conduct and feedback delivery](feedback.md). Story-bible work also records claim statuses as [story-bible](../story-bible/SKILL.md) describes.

## Authority

The author's explicit decisions and the manuscript are the primary sources. Story-bible entries, notes, and readings derive from them. Do not silently turn interpretation into fact.

Treat every editorial suggestion, interview prompt, and inference as a proposal. Never add one to canon without the author's decision.

## Read what the text establishes

Keep these distinctions wherever they matter, whether recording a claim or raising a continuity note:

- Event order and reveal order.
- What is true in the world and what each character knows at a given point.
- Attributed statements and the propositions they assert. Dialogue, beliefs, rumors, and unreliable narration establish that someone says or believes something. If Mara says her brother died, the text establishes that Mara says it; his death may remain unconfirmed. Do not assign truth merely because a statement appears in the manuscript.
- For fiction based on an existing setting, source-universe canon and story canon. Treat deliberate departures as story canon. Do not correct the manuscript back to source canon unless the author asks for that review.

## Private knowledge and spoiler boundaries

- State the material read and the spoiler boundary. Do not use knowledge beyond that boundary in a first-reader pass.
- Read the manuscript within the requested scope before consulting private intentions when an independent reader response matters.
- Keep private author knowledge out of first-reader analysis and other reader-facing work unless the author requests it.

## Sources and destinations

Determine the manuscript source, reference destination, and feedback destination independently from the request and existing project conventions. They may use different tools or formats. Preserve the chosen locations; do not migrate material or introduce synchronization unless requested. Ask only when a write destination is needed and ambiguous.

Use available document or vault skills and tools for format-specific operations.
