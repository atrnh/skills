# Editorial contract

Use this contract whenever a skill reads, records, or reviews fiction.

## Authority and provenance

The author's explicit decisions and the manuscript are the primary sources. The internal story bible records information derived from them. It does not silently turn interpretation into fact.

Record each claim with the narrowest applicable status:

- **Established:** Reliably established by the manuscript. Cite the source version, location, and passage.
- **Author decision:** Confirmed privately by the author, whether or not the manuscript reveals it.
- **Planned:** Intended but still changeable.
- **Possibility:** An option under consideration.
- **Inference:** A reading supported by the text but not confirmed.
- **Superseded:** Retained for history but no longer current.
- **Needs reassessment:** Previously supported, but its supporting evidence is no longer current. Preserve the former evidence; do not present the claim as currently established or disproved.
- **Conflict:** Two sources or claims require resolution. Preserve both until the author resolves them.

Status belongs to individual claims, not whole character or location pages. Keep event order separate from reveal order, and world truth separate from what each character knows at a given point.

Preserve attribution for dialogue, beliefs, rumors, and unreliable narration. If Mara says her brother died, the text establishes that Mara says it; his death may remain unconfirmed. Record the attributed statement separately from the underlying proposition when needed. Do not assign truth merely because a statement appears in the manuscript.

For fiction based on an existing setting, distinguish source-universe canon from story canon. Record deliberate departures. Do not correct the manuscript back to source canon unless the author asks for that review.

## Editorial conduct

- Read the manuscript within the requested scope before consulting private intentions when an independent reader response matters.
- State the material read and the spoiler boundary. Do not use knowledge beyond that boundary in a first-reader pass.
- Describe the reader effect before proposing a fix.
- Separate likely craft problems, continuity conflicts, and matters of taste.
- Preserve deliberate fragments, repetition, ambiguity, dialect, and unusual syntax unless they undermine the author's stated effect.
- Identify successful passages worth protecting when revision could disturb them.
- Do not manufacture praise or criticism to fill a quota.
- Treat every suggested change as a proposal. Never add a suggestion to canon automatically.

Reviews may leave comments according to the selected skill's delivery default, saved preferences, and the author's request. Rewriting manuscript text requires a separate explicit request.

## Sources and destinations

Determine the manuscript source, reference destination, and feedback destination independently from the request and existing project conventions. They may use different tools or formats. Preserve the chosen locations; do not migrate material or introduce synchronization unless requested. Ask only when a write destination is needed and ambiguous.

Use available document or vault skills and tools for format-specific operations. Do not assume an app supports comments or tracked changes. If the requested delivery is unavailable, explain the limitation and provide a located review in chat without claiming it was posted elsewhere.

For feedback delivery, read [feedback guidance](feedback.md). Load [Notion guidance](notion.md) only when posting or checking comments in Notion.
