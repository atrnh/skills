# Curriculum rubric

Version: 1

This rubric judges whether curriculum helps its learners understand and do what it sets out to
teach. It covers lectures, labs, exercises, assessments, content outlines, and whole courses.

| Code | Criterion | Gate |

| AC | Accuracy | yes |
| CO | Completeness | yes |
| AU | Audience fit | |

A gate criterion with a median below 7 means the material isn't ready, however well it scores
elsewhere.

## Scope

- **A lecture, lab, exercise, or assessment.** Read all of it, along with anything learners need
  from it, such as starter code, data files, or linked setup pages. Score every criterion that
  applies.
- **A content outline.** Judge the plan as the material it describes. Score accuracy,
  completeness of coverage, audience fit, objectives, progression, and the planned examples and
  practice. Language for purpose and presented form are usually `"na"`.
- **A whole course.** Read the course outline and index, then the units named for in-depth
  reading in the review's scope, and skim the rest. Judge progression and alignment across
  units as well as within them, and name the unit in every quote and finding.

## How to read

Read the material once in order as the learner described in the review's context, at their
pace. Note where you slow down, get lost, would need to search elsewhere, or stop trusting the
material. Then score each criterion, returning to the text for evidence.

## Criteria

### AC Accuracy (gate)

Facts, terminology, code, commands, interface paths, and claims are correct and current for the
tools and versions the material names.

- **Strong (9–10):** You found no errors. Code and commands would work as written, and claims are precise.
- **Sound (7–8):** Minor slips that a learner would recover from without being misled, such as a typo in
  a name that doesn't matter to any step.
- **Weak (4–6):** At least one error that would mislead learners or break a step, or many minor ones.
- **Failing (1–3):** Errors in core concepts, or in steps learners must complete.

If you can't run the code, judge it by reading and name what you couldn't verify in the
rationale. Answer `"cannot_assess"` only when accuracy depends entirely on something you can't
check.

### CO Completeness (gate)

Learners can understand and do what's asked without knowledge, setup, or resources that the
material neither supplies nor states as a prerequisite.

- **Strong (9–10):** Every step, term, prerequisite, and resource learners need is supplied or stated.
- **Sound (7–8):** Small gaps that learners at this level would fill without help.
- **Weak (4–6):** A gap that would stall many learners or send them to search elsewhere.
- **Failing (1–3):** Learners can't follow or complete the material as written.

### AU Audience fit

The level, assumed knowledge, pace, and tone suit the stated learners. The tone is friendly and
mentoring without talking down.

- **Strong (9–10):** Pitched for the stated learners throughout. New terms are defined where they first
  appear, and the tone encourages without padding.
- **Sound (7–8):** Mostly right, with a few passages that are too advanced, too basic, or off in tone.
- **Weak (4–6):** Repeatedly assumes knowledge the learners lack or labors what they already know, or
  uses a tone that would discourage them.
- **Failing (1–3):** Written for a different audience.

### OB Objectives and alignment

The material states or clearly implies what learners will be able to do afterward, and its
explanations, practice, and checks of understanding all serve those goals.

- **Strong (9–10):** The goals are clear, every section and activity serves them, and assessment items
  measure the goals rather than trivia.
- **Sound (7–8):** The goals are clear, but one or two parts drift from them, or an assessment item tests
  something peripheral.
- **Weak (4–6):** The goals are vague or missing, substantial content or practice serves none of them, or
  the assessment doesn't measure them.
- **Failing (1–3):** No goal can be discerned, or the material works against its stated goal.

### PR Progression

Each part has a reason to follow the one before it and a purpose in the work ahead.

- **Strong (9–10):** Each idea's prerequisites come before it. Transitions state how parts relate, and
  questions that would block understanding are answered before they do.
- **Sound (7–8):** A sound order with a weak transition, or a question left open longer than it should be.
- **Weak (4–6):** Learners must use ideas before they're taught, or related topics follow one another
  with no logic a learner could follow.
- **Failing (1–3):** The order prevents understanding.

A question left open on purpose, when resolving it is part of the learning, isn't a flaw.

### EX Examples and concepts

Examples, case studies, sample code, and demonstrations help learners recognize and use an idea
beyond the example itself.

- **Strong (9–10):** Each example has a stated purpose. Learners get the grounding to interpret it first,
  its details are tied to the concept and its terminology, and the reasoning transfers to other
  situations. Demonstrations stay focused on the cause and result they show.
- **Sound (7–8):** The examples work, but one lacks an explicit connection to its concept, or a
  demonstration runs longer than its teaching value.
- **Weak (4–6):** Learners could copy the examples without understanding why they work, or concepts that
  need an example have none.
- **Failing (1–3):** The examples mislead, or have no evident connection to the concepts.

Answer `"na"` only when the material's purpose calls for no examples.

### LA Language for purpose

Each passage's language matches its teaching purpose: explaining a concept, narrating a
demonstration, or directing practice.

- **Strong (9–10):** Explanations say what happens, why it matters, and how ideas connect. Demonstrations tie
  each step to its purpose and result. Practice instructions make the next action unmistakable.
- **Sound (7–8):** An occasional mismatch, such as an explanation that only lists steps.
- **Weak (4–6):** Frequent mismatches, such as instructions buried in explanation, or concepts delivered
  as commands with no reasoning.
- **Failing (1–3):** Learners can't tell what they're meant to understand or what they're meant to do.

### PD Practice design

Exercises, labs, and other practice spend learners' effort on the skill they're meant to
develop. Answer `"na"` when the material contains no practice.

- **Strong (9–10):** Learners make the decisions the skill requires, with starting points supplied where
  inventing them would be incidental work. The expected outcome and deliverable are concrete,
  and the intended challenge is intact.
- **Sound (7–8):** Mostly well designed, with some incidental work or a somewhat vague deliverable.
- **Weak (4–6):** Learners spend significant time on incidental work, would have to guess what counts as
  success, or are handed the reasoning they were meant to practice.
- **Failing (1–3):** The activity doesn't practice the intended skill, or can't be completed in the time
  available.

### PF Presented form

In slides, handouts, presenter notes, or another presented form, learners can follow the
reasoning at the point where they meet each idea. Answer `"na"` when there is no presented form.

- **Strong (9–10):** Each idea's premises appear before it in the form learners see. Related ideas stay
  together, breaks fall at meaningful shifts, and notes add depth without holding the essential
  connection.
- **Sound (7–8):** Mostly followable, with one awkward split or one connection that appears only in notes.
- **Weak (4–6):** Essential reasoning lives only in the source or the notes, or slides present conclusions
  without their premises.
- **Failing (1–3):** Learners can't follow the presented form.

When you can read only the source of the presented form, judge from its structure and say so in
the rationale.

## Maintaining this rubric

Everything above this heading goes to reviewers. Keep the criteria table's format, because
`scripts/aggregate.py` reads codes and gates from it. Raise the version whenever a change could
move scores, so that an improvement loop never compares runs scored against different rubrics.

These criteria turn this repository's education principles into scored descriptions. When a principle
changes, revise its criterion to match:

| Criterion | Principle |
| --- | --- |
| PR | principle-make-the-progression-meaningful |
| EX | principle-connect-examples-to-concepts |
| LA | principle-match-language-to-teaching-purpose |
| PD | principle-protect-practice-time |
| PF | principle-put-essential-reasoning-in-view |
