# Theory: functional quality vs deep quality

Paraphrased from Daniele Procida, "Towards a theory of quality in documentation", Diátaxis
(https://diataxis.fr/quality/). Read that page for the original argument.

## Why distinguish them

We rarely define "quality". Still, people can point to excellent documentation and tend to
agree when they see lapses. That shared intuition is worth sharpening. Splitting quality into
two kinds makes it something you can discuss and act on.

## Functional quality

Accuracy, completeness, consistency, usefulness, precision, and similar standards. Missing any
one of them means a key function is failing.

- The dimensions are **independent**. Writing can be accurate but incomplete. It can be
  complete but inaccurate and inconsistent. It can be all of these and still useless.
- They are **objective** and sometimes literally countable (completeness).
- Meeting them takes discipline, attention to detail, and technical skill. Any failure is
  **obvious to the reader**.

## Deep quality

Feeling good to use, having flow, fitting human needs, being beautiful, anticipating the user.

- You **can't check or measure** these, but you can clearly identify them. Most people
  recognise them when they meet them.
- Writing can pass every functional test and still be awkward and unpleasant. Accurate,
  consistent, even useful material can lack deep quality.

## How the two differ

| Functional quality | Deep quality |
|---|---|
| independent characteristics | interdependent characteristics |
| objective | subjective |
| measured against the world | assessed against the human |
| a condition of deep quality | conditional upon functional quality |
| experienced as constraint | experienced as liberation |

- **Interdependence:** flow and anticipating the user are aspects of each other. Nothing
  feels good to use unless it fits your needs.
- **Judgement, not measurement:** you question deep quality instead of measuring it.
- **Subjective:** you can only assess it against the needs of the person experiencing it.
- **Conditional:** no reader finds inaccurate writing beautiful, or enjoys how inconsistent
  writing anticipates their needs. One lapse spoils the experience.
- **Constraint vs liberation:** functional standards are tests you can fail, and you have to
  re-check them with every change to the thing being documented. Deep quality is creative
  work that takes taste. *You conform to reach functional quality. You invent to reach deep
  quality.*

## How deep quality is recognised: the clothing analogy

Clothes must keep you warm and dry and must last. Anyone can test those things. How a garment
hangs, moves, and is shaped takes a trained eye to judge. Yet anyone can tell that it's
excellent, because it feels good to wear, fits, and moves with them. The body knows.

Documentation works the same way. Readers may not be able to say why it's good. They may
confuse its obvious functional virtues with its deeper excellence. That doesn't matter. It
will feel good or it won't. **Creators**, though, need to recognise *what* makes it good, not
just *that* it is. They also need to understand how people will feel while using it. That
ability is what makes deep quality possible.

## What Diátaxis can and can't do

- **It can't supply functional quality.** That comes from craft and domain knowledge, and from
  gathering the whole terrain into one coherent, consistent map.
- **It can expose functional lapses** (an analytical role only):
  - Reference structure should mirror the structure of what it describes. This makes gaps
    visible.
  - Moving explanation out of a tutorial often reveals where the reader was left to work
    something out alone.
- **It can help create deep quality:**
  - *Fit:* its four modes (tutorial, how-to, reference, explanation) exist because of reader
    needs. Organising categories, arranging material, and choosing the form and language of
    each part are ways of fitting those needs.
  - *Flow:* flow is moving from one state to the next in a way that feels right and unforced,
    and in step with the reader's current concerns and with how minds and bodies work. Diátaxis
    protects flow by keeping out disruptions to purpose and progress, such as an explanatory
    digression inside a how-to guide.
- **Limits:** it can support beauty in overall form but can't create beauty on its own. It's a
  set of principles, not a formula, and not a shortcut past UX, interaction design, or visual
  design. It can't guarantee deep quality, which keeps being reinterpreted and reinvented. It
  creates the conditions that make deep quality possible.
