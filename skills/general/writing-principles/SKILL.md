# Writing principles

These principles help readers follow connected prose and find the answer that holds now. Apply the first whenever you draft or edit a passage. Apply the second when a document has to absorb decisions or changes made after it was written.

## Let context do its work

Write for a reader who has followed the explanation so far. Let established context carry repeated information, and spend new words on new meaning.

- Edit sentences in context. The surrounding passage determines what needs to be said.
- Carry established subjects forward naturally. Use shorter references when their meaning is clear.
- Cut repeated setup. Trust the reader to retain what the passage has established.
- Make missing connections explicit. Related facts still need an explanation of their relationship.
- Prefer familiar, concrete language. Fewer words should not require more decoding.

Concision depends on what the reader can reasonably infer. Repeat what prevents ambiguity or helps the reader regain their place. Preserve conditions, distinctions, and causal links that carry the meaning. This principle decides what the surrounding passage lets you leave unsaid. Word choice and sentence-level style rules belong to the document's style guide.

## Document the current state

Readers should not have to reconstruct the current answer from successive drafts. Rewrite the explanation around the decisions that now hold.

- Reconcile earlier statements with later decisions instead of appending another correction.
- Describe what exists and how to use it. Distinguish proposed work from implemented behavior.
- Make the entry point lead to the current deliverables and the information needed to use them.
- Keep rationale when it explains a present constraint or tradeoff.
- Give useful development history a clear place where readers can consult it without first reading it to understand the current state.

An experiment report, decision record, or change log may need its chronology intact. Preserve that evidence. The principle governs the current explanation and its relationship to history; it does not require deleting the history or making unfinished work sound complete.
