# Bound analysis by the decision

An analysis earns its effort by helping someone decide what to do. Define that decision before choosing how much information to collect or how elaborate the analysis should become.

- Identify the uncertainty that could change the decision.
- Start with the smallest useful sample or comparison, and expand where its findings require it.
- Treat difficulty classifying the evidence as a possible problem with the categories. Reconsider them before scaling the analysis.
- Stop when the evidence supports the next decision. Keep unresolved questions that could still change it visible.
- Build durable tooling when the analysis needs to be repeated. Let exploratory work remain disposable when its finding is the useful result.

The stopping point depends on the consequences of being wrong. A small sample cannot establish exhaustive coverage, and a decision with serious consequences may require stronger evidence. Explicit requests for a comprehensive inventory still require that coverage.
