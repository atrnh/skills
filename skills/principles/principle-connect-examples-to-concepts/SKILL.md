# Connect examples to concepts

An example should help learners recognize and use an idea beyond that example. Make the relationship between the concrete details and the underlying concept explicit.

- Establish what the example is meant to explain and why that matters.
- Introduce the categories or criteria an example depends on before asking learners to interpret it.
- Connect the example's choices and results to the concepts that explain them.
- Preserve useful terminology when making an explanation more concrete. Learners need language they can carry into another situation.
- Include the reasoning that makes the lesson transferable, rather than leaving learners with only a pattern to copy.
- Demonstrate where observing the behavior teaches more than explanation alone. Explain every idea the lesson needs, even those you do not demonstrate.
- Keep a demonstration focused enough that learners can see the relevant cause and result. Reuse a running example for continuity without forcing every topic to fit it.

An example can come first when it gives learners a useful question or something to investigate. Supply enough context for that purpose, then connect the observation to the concept. Neither abstract vocabulary alone nor an unexplained example provides that connection. A complete demonstration fits when performing the whole process is the learning goal; otherwise, judge its scope by the understanding it produces.
