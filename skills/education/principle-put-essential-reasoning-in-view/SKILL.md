# Put essential reasoning in view

An explanation can exist in the source and still be missing from the learner's experience. Preserve the premises and connections needed to follow the lesson in the form learners will encounter.

- Check what learners actually see and receive. Confirm that each idea's premises appear before it in that form.
- Keep related ideas together and use meaningful shifts to guide presentation breaks.
- Use supporting material for elaboration without hiding the connection that makes the main content understandable.
- Judge pacing and fit in the presented result. Source structure alone cannot show whether an idea has been split awkwardly or lost.

The presented material need not reproduce every spoken explanation or supporting detail. Keep the essential relationship visible and use narration or notes for depth. When space or accessibility requires a break, preserve enough continuity for learners to follow it.
