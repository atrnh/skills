# Let demonstrations serve the lesson

A demonstration earns its place by making an idea easier to understand. Choose what to show from the learning goal and the learner's likely misconceptions.

- Demonstrate behavior that is difficult to understand through explanation alone.
- Keep the example focused enough that learners can see the relevant cause and result.
- Reuse a running example when it supports continuity, without forcing every topic to fit it.
- Explain important ideas even when demonstrating all of them would distract from the lesson.

A complete demonstration may be necessary when performing the whole process is the learning goal. Conversely, a small demonstration can be enough for a conceptual point. Judge scope by the understanding it produces, without treating every demonstration as a complete project. This principle decides whether and how much to demonstrate. How to narrate the demonstration and connect what it shows to the concept belongs to the guidance on teaching language and examples.
